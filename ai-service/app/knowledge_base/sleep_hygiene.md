Sleep hygiene refers to the habits and environment that support consistent, restorative sleep — and poor sleep is one of the biggest amplifiers of anxiety and low mood.

Key practices:
- Keep a consistent sleep and wake time, even on weekends.
- Avoid screens (phones, laptops, TV) for 30–60 minutes before bed; blue light suppresses melatonin.
- Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and used only for sleep (not work or scrolling).
- Avoid caffeine after early afternoon and heavy meals or alcohol close to bedtime.
- If you can't sleep after ~20 minutes, get up and do something calm in dim light rather than lying there frustrated — this prevents the brain from associating the bed with wakefulness.
- A short wind-down routine (light stretching, reading, breathing exercises) signals to the body that sleep is coming.

Improving sleep is often one of the highest-leverage changes for overall emotional regulation.
