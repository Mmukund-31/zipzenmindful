Gratitude journaling is one of the most researched positive-psychology interventions, consistently linked to improved mood, better sleep, and reduced symptoms of depression when practiced regularly.

How to do it well:
- Write down 3 specific things you're grateful for each day — specificity matters more than quantity ("the way my coworker covered for me this morning" beats "my job").
- Include at least one entry involving another person; gratitude toward people has a stronger mood effect than gratitude toward things.
- Briefly note *why* it mattered, not just what it was.
- Consistency beats intensity: a few minutes daily outperforms a long entry once a week.

The mechanism is attentional: regularly scanning for good things trains the brain to notice them more automatically over time, counteracting the negativity bias that stress and anxiety amplify.
