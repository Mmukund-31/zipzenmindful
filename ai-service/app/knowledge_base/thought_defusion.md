---
title: Thought Defusion
category: cbt
tags: overthinking, intrusive-thoughts, act
---
Thought defusion, from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), helps create distance from unhelpful thoughts instead of arguing with them or trying to force them away — useful when a thought is repetitive but not really "solvable."

Techniques to try:
- Add the prefix "I'm having the thought that..." before the thought (e.g., "I'm having the thought that I'm going to fail" instead of "I'm going to fail"). This small linguistic shift reminds the brain that a thought is a mental event, not a fact.
- Say the thought slowly in a silly voice, or sing it to a simple tune — this sounds odd but reliably reduces a thought's emotional grip.
- Picture the thought as a leaf floating down a stream or a cloud passing in the sky, watching it drift rather than grabbing onto it.

The goal isn't to eliminate the thought, but to change your relationship to it so it has less power over your mood and actions.
