title: Anger Management Basics
category: anger
tags: anger, frustration, regulation
---
Anger itself isn't the problem — it's a normal signal, often pointing to a boundary crossed or a need unmet. The goal isn't to suppress it but to create enough space to respond rather than react.

In the moment:
- Notice early physical cues (heat, clenched jaw, faster heartbeat) before anger peaks — it's much easier to manage at a 3/10 than a 9/10.
- Use a brief pause: step away if possible, take 5 slow breaths, or count to 10 before speaking or acting.
- Lower the physical intensity first (cold water on your face/wrists, a short walk) before trying to think your way out of it — a flooded nervous system can't reason well.

Afterward:
- Ask what the anger was actually protecting or pointing to (a boundary, a value, an unmet need).
- If it involved another person, decide what you want to say once you're calm, rather than in the heat of the moment.
